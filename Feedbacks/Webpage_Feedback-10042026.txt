I went through the live KENIS WCS website⁠ , including the homepage, Capabilities, Platform, AI Operations, Analytics, Expertise, and Contact pages. Your instinct is right: the underlying content is strong, but there is too much of it presented at the same level of importance. That makes KENIS feel more complicated than it needs to.

What I would change

1. Simplify the top navigation.

Right now you have:

Capabilities | Platform | Deployment | AI Operations | Analytics | Expertise | Contact

That’s too many primary choices, especially because AI, analytics, deployment, and platform capabilities overlap.

I would change it to:

Solutions | Platform | Why KENIS | About | Contact

Then put AI Operations, Analytics, Deployment, Implementation, Testing, and HyperCare underneath Solutions.

⸻

2. Make the homepage much shorter.

The homepage currently introduces WMS/WCS orchestration, AI, end-to-end delivery, six separate capability cards, your Plan → Integrate → Automate → Test → Launch → Optimize process, four benefits, and another CTA. 

That’s a lot for someone who has known KENIS for 30 seconds.

I would make the homepage tell one simple story:

Headline

Connect Your WMS, WCS and Warehouse Automation.

Subheadline

KENIS integrates warehouse systems, automation and operational intelligence through one flexible orchestration platform.

Then three simple pillars:

Integrate
Connect WMS, WCS and automation.

Operate
Monitor workflows, exceptions and performance.

Optimize
Use analytics and AI to identify problems and improve operations.

Then:

One Partner. From Integration to Go-Live.

And one strong CTA:

Talk to a KENIS Expert

That’s enough.

⸻

3. Your Platform page needs the biggest reduction.

This is where I see the strongest “clustered” problem.

The page currently has architecture, API Gateway, business rules, workflow orchestration, event processing, exception handling, inventory synchronization, equipment coordination, monitoring, audit, operations features, IT features, nine separate error-handling features, reliability, SLAs, three service tiers, eight configurable modules, scaling, warehouse scenarios, engineering principles, and business outcomes. 

It’s excellent material for a technical proposal or RFP.

It’s too much for the public website.

I’d cut roughly 50–60% of this page.

The Platform page should primarily show:

WMS / ERP / OMS
↓
KENIS Integration & Orchestration Platform
↓
WCS / PLC / Conveyors / Sorters / Robotics

And underneath that, six capabilities:

Integration
Orchestration
Exception Management
Inventory Synchronization
Monitoring
Analytics & AI

Everything else can eventually become technical documentation, a downloadable solution brief, or sales material.

⸻

4. Don’t explain the same service on multiple pages.

For example, AI Operations appears on the homepage, Capabilities page, Platform page, and its own AI page. Analytics follows a similar pattern. 

Repeating a mention is good. Repeating the explanation makes the website feel larger and more complicated.

Use this rule:

Homepage = What KENIS does
Solutions = What problems you solve
Platform = How the technology works
Why KENIS = Why customers should trust you

⸻

5. Keep the AI page — but simplify it.

This is actually one of your better pages because it has a clear story:

Detect → Diagnose → Correct → Recover. 

Keep that.

But I would reduce the long use-case list and focus on four:

Detect failed/missing messages
Identify root causes
Recommend corrections
Safely reprocess approved transactions

And keep “AI recommends. Your team stays in control.” That is a strong enterprise message. 

⸻

6. Keep Analytics visual.

Your Analytics page has something your other pages don’t: an actual simulated operational dashboard showing UCH, RCH, attainment, projected finish and zone performance. 

That’s valuable.

Instead of explaining Analytics extensively, let the dashboard sell it.

Headline:

Know Before Your Operation Falls Behind.

Then dashboard.

Then three benefits:

Real-time throughput
Early performance alerts
Projected completion & cutoff risk

Much cleaner.

⸻

The biggest messaging change

Right now KENIS is trying to tell visitors everything you can do.

I want it to tell visitors:

What problem do we solve?

Warehouse systems don’t always communicate cleanly.

What does KENIS do?

We connect and orchestrate WMS, WCS and warehouse automation.

Why does that matter?

Fewer integration problems, faster issue resolution, better visibility and less disruption.

What do I do next?

Talk to KENIS.

That should be understandable in 10–15 seconds.

I would also change the positioning slightly

Your current homepage leads with the technically accurate but long phrase “warehouse integration and orchestration platform between WMS and WCS.” 

I’d make KENIS sound more outcome-oriented:

Warehouse Automation. Connected.

KENIS connects WMS, WCS and automation through one intelligent orchestration platform—helping operations integrate faster, recover from problems sooner and see what’s happening across the floor.

Then your existing “Warehouse Automation. Integrated. Intelligent.” can remain as the brand tagline in the footer. 

My recommended new website structure

I would reduce the whole site to 5 main sections:

HOME → SOLUTIONS → PLATFORM → WHY KENIS → CONTACT

Under Solutions, you can cover WCS Integration | Project Delivery | Testing & Go-Live | AI Operations | Analytics.

Under Platform, cover Architecture | Integration | Orchestration | Reliability | Deployment.

That structure would make KENIS look more established, easier to understand, and less like a technical specification document.

And importantly, I would not remove the technical material permanently. A company evaluating KENIS—especially an enterprise customer—may eventually want that detail. I would move the deeper architecture/SLA/error-handling material into solution briefs, technical PDFs, or sales/RFP documentation rather than putting all of it in the main browsing experience.
